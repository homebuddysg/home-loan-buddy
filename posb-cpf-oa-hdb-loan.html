HDB Concessionary Loan vs POSB / DBS CPF-OA Package: My View as a New HDB Buyer

If I were buying a new HDB flat, which loan would give me the balance of lower repayments, transparency and peace of mind?

Published: 24 September 2026 · Last checked: 24 September 2026 · Reading time: 8 minutes

My short answerIf I were buying a new HDB flat today, I would find the POSB / DBS CPF-OA HDB loan package worth considering because its published rates are below the HDB Concessionary Loan rate under the current assumptions. However, I would not call it “2.40% fixed forever”. It is linked to CPF-OA, so I would confirm the rate formula and full package terms before making a decision.

One of my biggest concerns as a new HDB buyer would be repayment certainty. With economic conditions and interest rates difficult to predict, I would want a loan structure that gives me peace of mind rather than one that looks attractive only during a short introductory period.

My first instinct might be to consider the HDB Concessionary Loan. Its 2.60% p.a. rate has remained unchanged for a long time, which may lead many borrowers to think of it as effectively fixed. However, the important detail is that the rate is actually pegged at 0.10 percentage points above the prevailing CPF Ordinary Account (CPF-OA) interest rate. [SOURCE: HDB official interest-rate page]

That is why the POSB / DBS CPF-OA HDB loan package caught my attention. Under the published assumptions, it starts at 1.78% p.a. for the first three years, moves to 2.20% p.a. for Years 4–5 and is then shown at 2.40% p.a. thereafter. My initial reaction is that this looks more transparent and reassuring than a conventional package that begins with a fixed period and then moves to a SORA-linked rate.

That is my personal impression, not a recommendation. The package is linked to CPF-OA and should not be described as 2.40% fixed forever. Its appeal, in my view, comes from the stated relationship with CPF-OA, the lower published rates under the current assumptions and the additional flexibility described in the package terms.

How the two loan structures work

The HDB Concessionary Loan and the POSB / DBS CPF-OA Package use different formulas. Understanding that difference is more important than simply comparing two headline rates.

LoanReference structurePublished rate under current assumptions
HDB Concessionary LoanCPF-OA + 0.10 percentage points2.60% p.a.
POSB / DBS CPF-OA PackageCPF-OA less a stated discount during each period1.78% p.a. for Years 1–3; 2.20% p.a. for Years 4–5; 2.40% p.a. thereafter

When CPF-OA is 2.50% p.a., the difference between the HDB rate and the POSB thereafter rate is:

2.60% − 2.40% = 0.20 percentage points
This is a rate difference, not a guaranteed cash saving of 0.20% of the original loan every year. Interest is calculated on the outstanding balance, which normally declines over time.

I find that easier to understand conceptually, although I would still check exactly how the rate is determined in the letter of offer.

What could the repayment difference look like?

The POSB page illustrates the package using a S$400,000 loan repaid monthly over 20 years. It states that the package could save about S$3,100 a year during the three-year lock-in period compared with the HDB Concessionary Loan, subject to the stated assumptions. [SOURCE: POSB official promotion page]

That figure should not be treated as a universal saving. The actual result will depend on the loan amount, tenure, outstanding balance, rate assumptions, package conditions, fees and the timing of repayments.

What I like about the package

1. The rate structure is easier to follow

Personally, I find a stated relationship with CPF-OA easier to understand than a package that changes from a fixed rate to SORA after a short period.

2. The published rates are lower under current assumptions

The initial 1.78% and later 2.20% and 2.40% figures are below the 2.60% HDB comparison rate while CPF-OA is 2.50% p.a.

3. It may offer more peace of mind

For a buyer who values predictability, the package’s stated rate formula may feel more transparent than relying only on a short fixed-rate period.

4. There are package-flexibility features

The published page highlights a one-time free switch after three years and other conditions. I would still verify the exact terms before relying on them.

What makes me cautious

This is not a permanently fixed 2.40% rate.
The package remains linked to CPF-OA. If the applicable reference rate or package terms change, the rate may change. I would therefore avoid presenting the package as “fixed forever” in any comparison.
  • Promotional rates and rewards may have deadlines and eligibility conditions.
  • Fees, subsidies and clawback conditions can affect the real cost.
  • A lower rate does not automatically mean a lower total cost.
  • The borrower’s loan amount, tenure and outstanding balance affect the actual savings.
  • The full letter of offer matters more than a summary table or advertisement.

Who might want to consider it?

In my view, the package may be worth researching for an HDB buyer who values a clear reference-rate structure, wants to compare something other than the traditional HDB loan and is comfortable with the three-year commitment and the package conditions.

I would be more cautious if I wanted a genuinely fixed rate with no reference-rate movement, expected to sell or refinance soon, planned to make a large early repayment or was relying on promotional rewards without checking the qualifying terms.

Questions I would ask before applying

  1. Is the rate formula clearly stated in the letter of offer?
  2. What happens to the applicable rate if CPF-OA changes?
  3. What are the exact conditions for switching packages after three years?
  4. Are there clawbacks for any cash reward or fee subsidy?
  5. Are there fees for partial repayment or early redemption?
  6. What happens if I sell or refinance during the commitment period?
  7. What rate and package apply if I do not exercise the package switch?
  8. Are the advertised rates still available when I apply?

Frequently asked questions

Is the POSB / DBS package fixed forever?

No. The published schedule provides numerical rates for the stated periods under the current assumptions, but the package is linked to CPF-OA. It should not be described as a permanently fixed 2.40% p.a. rate.

Is the HDB Concessionary Loan fixed forever?

No. HDB states that its concessionary rate is pegged at 0.10 percentage points above the prevailing CPF-OA interest rate. Its rate may remain unchanged for a long period, but the structure is formula-based.

Does the 0.20 percentage-point difference equal my cash savings?

No. It is the difference between the two stated rates under the current assumptions. The actual dollar difference depends on the outstanding balance, repayment schedule, tenure, fees and future applicable rates.

Can I use the calculator for a personal loan decision?

The calculator is an educational illustration only. Use it to understand the mechanics, then verify current terms and obtain an official quotation before deciding.

My conclusion

My view is that the POSB / DBS CPF-OA HDB loan package is worth considering for an HDB buyer who values a clear rate formula and wants to compare something other than the traditional HDB loan or a short fixed-rate package followed by SORA. I find the stated CPF-OA relationship reassuring because it is relatively easy to explain.

At the same time, I would not describe it as a guaranteed 2.40% fixed rate forever. The rate structure remains linked to CPF-OA, and the full package terms, eligibility requirements, promotional conditions and future rate changes still matter. I would use the calculator as a starting point, then confirm the current offer directly with the bank before making a decision.
